## Brief

Margin is a small offline-capable journal for books and long articles. Each
reading record contains a title, author, current place, and a stream of
entries. An entry can be an excerpt, an observation, or a question. That is
the whole vocabulary; no scores, reading streaks, or completion graphs are
required before a book can matter.

The project began after finding useful lines scattered across paper slips,
phone screenshots, and unsearchable notebook pages. The aim was not to build
a comprehensive reading manager. It was to make one modest habit dependable:
capture a thought quickly, then find it again in the context that gave it
meaning.

## Product decisions

### Capture without interruption

The entry composer stays to one small panel. A keyboard shortcut opens it, the
cursor begins in the text field, and the last-used entry kind remains selected.
Page number and chapter are optional because stopping to retrieve precise
metadata is often enough friction to lose the thought itself.

An excerpt can be followed immediately by a reflection, but the interface
does not require commentary. Sometimes saving the words accurately is the
useful act; interpretation can arrive on a later reading.

### A library arranged by return

The home view favors books with recent unanswered questions and recently added
notes rather than an artificial percentage complete. Finished books remain
easy to reach but no longer occupy the same attention as the text currently
being lived with.

Search matches titles, authors, excerpts, and reflections. Results remain
grouped beneath their source book so a phrase is never separated from where it
was encountered.

## Technical shape

The application is intentionally local-first. A compact IndexedDB store holds
the collection on the device, and every record can be exported as Markdown
and JSON for ownership and portability. No account is required to start, and
the export format is plain enough to remain readable without the app.

```ts
type JournalEntry = {
  kind: "excerpt" | "reflection" | "question";
  text: string;
  location?: string;
  createdAt: string;
};
```

The first prototype used a separate table for highlights and notes. In use,
the separation made a reading session feel fragmented, because an excerpt and
its response appeared in different places. A single chronological entry
stream proved simpler to understand and easier to export.

## Accessibility and resilience

Every entry kind is identified by text in addition to color. The complete
capture flow works by keyboard, with visible focus and an announcement after
an entry is saved. Offline operation is treated as the normal state rather
than a degraded one; the journal opens and accepts writing without a network
connection.

Backups are intentionally visible. A quiet export reminder appears after the
collection has changed substantially since its last download. Local-first is
only trustworthy when local loss has been considered honestly.

## Result

Margin has remained useful because it avoids rewarding accumulation. It does
not celebrate a tall stack of books or make unfinished reading appear as debt.
It gives each text a calm place for marks in the margin, then makes those
marks available when a later question sends me searching back through them.

The next iteration will explore optional device-to-device import using an
encrypted file transfer. It will remain an optional convenience, not a reason
for the core journal to require an account or surrender readable exports.
