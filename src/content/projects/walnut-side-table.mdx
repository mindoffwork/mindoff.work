## Working specification

- **Overall size:** 460 mm wide x 360 mm deep x 510 mm high.
- **Material:** American black walnut, selected for continuous grain across the
  top and shelf.
- **Joinery:** Mortise-and-tenon leg and apron frame; housed shelf supports.
- **Finish:** Three thin coats of hardwax oil, rubbed back between coats.
- **Constraint:** Solid wood throughout, with no visible hardware.

> The goal was not a statement piece. It was a useful object that grows quieter
> once it is in the room.

## Design decisions

### A softer top

The top carries a generous corner radius and a small underside bevel. From
standing height it reads as a thin, light plane; at hand height it has no sharp
edge to catch a knee. The overhang is deliberately short so the legs still
feel connected to the top rather than hidden below it.

### Storage without weight

A full drawer added complexity and made the small footprint feel boxed in.
The open shelf holds the current book and leaves daylight visible through the
frame. It sits high enough for cleaning underneath and low enough to keep the
tabletop clear.

## Build sequence

### 01 / Stock and grain

Boards were rough cut oversize, allowed to settle, then milled to final
thickness on a second pass. The clearest grain became the top; quieter pieces
became aprons and legs so the eye lands on the surface first.

### 02 / Frame and dry fit

The leg blanks were tapered after the mortises were cut, keeping every
reference face square during joinery. A full dry fit exposed a small twist in
one apron, which was remade before glue-up rather than pulled into alignment
under clamp pressure.

### 03 / Shelf and edges

The lower shelf is captured on discreet supports inside the frame, leaving a
small expansion gap across the grain. Edges were routed, then hand softened so
the radii do not look mechanically identical at every corner.

### 04 / Finish

After sanding to 180 grit, the table was wiped with mineral spirits to check
for glue marks and scratches. Hardwax oil was applied in thin passes, leaving a
low sheen that protects the walnut without turning the grain glossy.

## Result

The finished table is stable on hard flooring, easy to lift with one hand, and
large enough for the daily objects it was designed around. The shelf proved to
be the most useful choice: it gives a paperback a home while preserving the
simple top surface.

The next iteration would reduce the apron depth slightly and prototype an even
lighter shelf edge. Neither change is necessary for this piece to work, but
both would make the silhouette a little calmer.
