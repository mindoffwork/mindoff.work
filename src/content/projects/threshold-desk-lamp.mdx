## Material specification

- **Base:** Ash block with an internal steel weight and felt underside.
- **Arm:** Powder-coated aluminium tube with a concealed low-voltage cable.
- **Shade:** Spun aluminium cone with a matte ivory inner surface.
- **Light:** Warm-white high-CRI LED module behind a frosted diffuser.
- **Control:** Knurled brass rotary dimmer placed at the front-right edge.

The lamp began with a particular annoyance: a desk bright enough for calls and
screens felt harsh once work ended, while switching to the room's ambient
light made reading difficult. I wanted one object to mark that change in pace
through a physical gesture rather than a phone control or a lighting scene.

## Light before form

The first tests were not drawings of a lamp. They were pieces of diffusion
material clipped above a desk at different heights. A narrow source made a
beautiful object shadow but an unpleasant reading surface; a wide diffuser
softened handwriting, paper, and keyboard together.

That experiment set the shade dimensions before the arm shape was decided.
The low cone shields the LED from seated eye level while leaving a broad pool
across an open notebook. Its pale inner finish reflects warmth without
introducing the yellow cast of an exposed timber shade.

## A control found by hand

Touch controls disappeared visually, but they also disappeared at the moment
they were needed. In a dim room I had to search for a mark on the base and
guess whether a long press would brighten or dim. A rotary knob made the
interaction immediate and gave the lamp a natural resting state between full
working light and a lower evening level.

The brass knob is slightly cooler in color than the ash base and deeply
knurled at its edge. It can be found without looking and turned with one
finger while holding a page open with the other hand.

## Build notes

The ash base was hollowed from underneath to accept both the steel plate and
the dimmer enclosure, then closed with a removable felt-covered panel.
Low-voltage wiring runs through the aluminium arm, isolated from the rotating
control and strain-relieved where it meets the shade.

The original arm used two pivot joints. Although flexible, it made the lamp
look permanently unsettled and added places for the cable to fatigue. The
finished fixed arm places light where the desk needs it; moving the object
itself is easier than preserving adjustment that was rarely used.

## Finish and use

Ash received a water-based matte finish so its pale grain stays close to the
paper and fawn surfaces around it. The metal arm is a soft dark charcoal,
strong enough to frame the light without becoming the visual center of the
desk. Felt beneath the weighted base lets it move intentionally without
scratching the surface.

After several months the lamp still performed its simplest ritual well: turn
down the light, close the work, open a book. The project is archived because
the object no longer asks for refinement. Its success is not that it does many
things, but that one turn of a knob makes the same desk belong to a different
part of the day.
